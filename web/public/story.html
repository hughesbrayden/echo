01 The problem

For blind and low-vision people, a cane only tells part of the story.

A cane finds what's on the ground about a step ahead. Everything higher, or a few steps further, arrives without warning.

The question

How might we tell the rest of the story, without expensive specialist hardware?

02 The solution

Echo, a lookout you can wear.

Echo is an agentic navigation system built for video glasses. As you walk, it shares important details about your surroundings to help you navigate with confidence.

03 What sets us apart

Today you get effective or affordable. Echo is both.

Tools that help blind and low-vision people get around already exist. The ones that work well while you walk cost thousands or mean wearing a clunky rig, and the affordable ones wait to be asked or only beep.

04 How we built it

Five steps, twice a second.

The phone sees and speaks. A small server asks a decision model a few navigation questions about each look, and plain code turns the answers into silence, words or the alarm.

Why a decision model

A chat model gives you a sentence. A decision model gives you a number you can tune.

Each question comes back as a probability, so "should Echo speak?" becomes a threshold we can test against recorded walks instead of a prompt we hope behaves.

It only charges for input, about a cent per minute of walking. Estimate
